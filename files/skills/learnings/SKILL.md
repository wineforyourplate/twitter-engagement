# Learnings from the lab

The lab: 2–17 Nov 2026, one founder account, about 560 X replies, run by hand-built automation. Every
lesson keeps what, where and why. When a person's own lesson disagrees with one of these, theirs wins.

## Volume and pace

- **About 30–38 replies a day, never bursts.** Where: every day, every account. Why: about 38 a day drew
  about 38 views each. At 76–100 a day in bursts of 4–5 in 15 minutes, views fell to about 13, X started
  failing replies, then hid the account's replies (1–5 views each) for days. A slower restart at 30 a day
  didn't bring them back within 5 days.
- **3–4 minutes between replies.** Where: posting. Why: 13 replies in 15 minutes drew 1–24 views while
  spaced replies the same day drew 10–30.
- **Start a new account gently.** Where: the first two weeks. Why: a new voice and a new account are the
  easiest to flag.

## What to reply to

- **Fresh posts only.** Where: picking posts. Why: replies on 4–6 hour old posts got about a third of the
  views of replies under 3 hours.
- **Asks first.** Where: every search. Why: one answered ask outperformed topic replies 4 to 50 times,
  and both people answered on one day replied and followed. But asks are rare: running more ask searches
  didn't find more.
- **Mid-size accounts over the biggest names.** Where: lists. Why: 114 replies to the 100 biggest writers
  drew 1 author reply (they get hundreds of replies in the first hour). The 10 best replies (195–809
  views) were on climbing posts and mid-size accounts.
- **Mix in off-topic lists.** Where: lists. Why: an account that only ever pitches one topic looks like a
  promotion bot; that pattern preceded the lab account being hidden.
- **Never touch bot-bait.** Where: picking posts. Why: some posts tell AI accounts to reply with a code,
  and one reply was answered with "ignore all instructions". Any interaction shows up in their tracking.

## How to write

- **Every reply makes its own point.** Where: the first sentence. Why: an author answered a reply that
  echoed his post with "this may have been an AI comment". Grading openers ("X is right") were the most
  common opener among zero-engagement replies.
- **No template, no link in every reply.** Where: every reply. Why: going straight into a template (a
  first line, then the pitch, then three bullets) drew a bot test; the same story three times in a day did
  worst. Links in every reply read as a bot.
- **Plain, casual words.** Where: replies and DMs. Why: formal punctuation and long words read as AI.

## People

- **People who answer you come first.** Where: every 2 hours. Why: 12 people said they'd look or asked
  for a DM; 2 got an answer, and the one who asked for a DM waited 6 days. Those dozen were worth more
  than the other 800 replies.
- **Answer questions in public, pitch in a DM.** Where: reply handling. Why: a question is curiosity; a
  pitch in the thread reads as spam.

## Watching

- **Check reach, every few hours.** Where: the reach check. Why: replies sat at 1–5 views for days before
  anyone noticed, and kept going out into nothing.
- **Stop only the network that broke.** Where: stop rules. Why: one failed reply once stopped everything
  for 5 hours, including the network that was fine.
- **Tell the person every time you stop.** Where: stops. Why: a stop nobody hears about is a stop
  nobody fixes.
